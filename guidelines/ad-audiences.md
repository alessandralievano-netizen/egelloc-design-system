# egelloC — Webinar Ad Creative: Cold vs Warm

Addendum to the **egelloC Marketing Design System** (`DESIGN-SYSTEM-COMPLETE.md`).
Derived from the 2026 webinar briefs (January–September 2026).

**Two readers:**

- **Claude Design** — use this to decide *how* a webinar static or carousel should look and behave once you know whether it is cold or warm. Read Parts 1, 2 and 2B.
- **The brief writer** — use this to write briefs that Claude Design (or a designer) can execute on the first pass. Read Parts 2, 2B and 3. The full brief-writing procedure (strategy layer, ASCII sketches, deliverables, .md + .pdf output) is in `guidelines/creative-brief.md`; this file and that one agree on every rule below.

---

## 0. Precedence — read first

This file does not define a single colour, font, size, radius or logo rule. The Design System does.

1. **The Design System wins on everything visual and structural**: tokens, Inter 400/800, the `static-card` template, logo hierarchy, legibility floors, the credibility line, the anti-cheap checks.
2. **This file wins on audience decisions**: which ground to pick, how much to explain, what Tony does, what the hook is allowed to do, how a carousel is sequenced, whether the job is a new concept or an iteration.
3. **Persona sets register and claims.** Temperature decides the ad's job; persona (Part 2B) decides vocabulary, proof and emotional register within it.
4. **The brief wins on copy.** Copy in a brief is set verbatim (Design System: "Copy is the user's call").

**Guardrails, not a box.** The Design System defaults stay in force across every format and apply whenever a brief is silent; a briefed departure is a per-brief exception, not a change to the defaults. The marketing team tests new things, so when a brief **explicitly asks** for something that departs from a default (all-caps headline, full-width rectangle CTA, no credibility line on a warm unit, a 9:16 static, a new device), build it as briefed. Keep what makes it egelloC: palette tokens, Inter, logo integrity, legibility and contrast floors, the anti-cheap polish checks. List each departure in one line in the delivery note so a winning test can be promoted into the system. Defaults apply only where the brief is silent.

**Coach photography.** Coach Tony (Tony Le, founder, former UC Berkeley admissions reader) is a different person from Coach David. Tony photos: `assets/photography/coach-tony-arms-crossed-cutout.png` (transparent, use for photo treatments) and `coach-tony-arms-crossed-wood.png` (wood-wall background, use as a contained photo, never as a cut-out). Never substitute a David photo where a brief calls for Tony.

Briefs describe colour by **role** (dark / light / brand ground, accent, emphasis). Claude Design maps each role to the tokens in §4. If a brief names a hex value, translate it with the table in §4 — never introduce a new hex.

# PART 1 — What "cold" and "warm" mean here

**Cold and warm describe the audience, not the mood.**

| | Cold | Warm |
|---|---|---|
| Who | California parents (mainly of 9th–10th graders) who have never engaged with egelloC | Facebook/Instagram engagers who already know egelloC and Coach Tony (the 2026 warm audience was ~650K engagers) |
| Awareness stage | Problem-aware at best. Often unaware the problem exists | Product-aware. Know the brand, the credential, the offer |
| What they're asking | "Is this for me? Who is this person? Why should I care?" | "Is this one worth my 90 minutes?" |
| The ad's job | Qualify → earn trust → open a loop → drive registration | Show the payoff → open a curiosity loop → drive registration |

> ⚠️ **"Warm" is also used in the briefs to describe tone** — e.g. a cold ad on a cream ground with a "warm, reassuring" register. That is still a **cold** ad. Every brief must state the audience temperature explicitly in its header (see Part 3), so the word never has to be guessed from context.

### How the 2026 briefs were classified

| Temperature | Briefs |
|---|---|
| **Cold webinar** | Cold Webinar Static Briefs (7 scripts) · Admissions Webinar Static Ads (June) · July Admissions Statics & Carousels · 4-Year Roadmap Creative Brief (July) + Round 1 · Competitive Families Static Briefs + CF Image & Carousel Brief ("Beyond the GPA") · Summer Planning statics · Majors Webinar Round 1* · Winner Ads Fast Iterations (`Live_Webinar_*`)* |
| **Warm webinar** | The Acceptance Moment (June) · The Pattern carousel iterations (June) |
| **Source concepts for warm** | Case Studies Webinar Image & Carousel Brief ("5 Families"): written for all personas, but the two warm winners (Acceptance Moment = Image Concept 8, The Pattern = Carousel 4) came from it |
| **Not webinars — out of scope** | 9/10 Grade Guide (×3) · UC GPA Calculator (×3) · UC Guide · Essays Vault · College List Pressure Test. These are lead magnets; most cold rules below transfer, but they weren't used to derive this file |

\* Audience not stated in the brief — assumed cold. **Confirm.**

**Honest limit:** the warm sample is two briefs from one webinar (the 5-families case studies). The warm rules below are what those two briefs consistently do; treat them as a starting pattern, not settled law, until more warm campaigns exist.

# PART 2 — How they differ

## 1. Side-by-side

| Dimension | Cold | Warm |
|---|---|---|
| **Kind of brief** | Mostly *new concepts*: new angles, new hooks, 2 treatments per concept | Mostly *iterations of a proven winner*: visual locked, only copy zones change |
| **First 2 seconds must say** | Who it's for + what topic (California parents · 9th/10th · UC / college admissions) | The outcome (acceptances, schools, "5 families got in") |
| **Audience qualifier / eyebrow** | Yes, almost always ("FOR CALIFORNIA PARENTS OF 9TH & 10TH GRADERS") — or the headline itself names the audience and topic | No. They already know who it's for |
| **Credibility** | Load-bearing. Credibility line / badge on every unit; credential often inside the copy ("I read 10,000 applications at UC Berkeley") | Light. Results carry the trust; Tony's face carries the rest |
| **Proof type** | Insider credential, shocking stat, the reader's process (8 minutes, 2 vs 6, 60 seconds) | Outcomes: school names, acceptance letters, the five families |
| **Coach Tony** | Credibility cue, not hero. In carousels he appears *late* — the answer to the question the earlier cards created (usually card 4 of 5). Text-only treatment is always briefed alongside the photo treatment | The familiar face on the CTA card. Trust anchor at the moment of action |
| **Emotional register** | Chosen per ad: hard truth, quiet dread, insider mystery, recognition, reassurance. Discomfort is allowed — it must resolve | Hope + curiosity. Aspirational, celebratory but restrained. No fear agitation |
| **Hook moves** | Myth-bust · stat shock · insider mystery · regret/time · recognition scene · objection reversal | Surprise reversal ("broken profiles → they all got in") · outcome-first ("Stanford said yes") |
| **Default ground** | Picked by emotional job (see §2). Dark grounds are common | Light: Off White, flat. No gradient, no texture |
| **Visual hero** | The headline. Typography-first. At most one supporting device (a stat, a transcript, a clock, a folder) | Headline + one emotional illustration (the envelope) *or* pure typography |
| **Payoff line** | Emphasis on 1–3 words inside the headline | A distinct italic-bold reversal/payoff line beneath the list |
| **CTA wording** | "Save your free seat" · "Get the free training" · "Register free" · "Show me how (free)" | "Free live training" · "Register free" |
| **Open loop** | Never answer the "how" — name it, tease it, don't explain it | Never resolve "what happened" — show the result, withhold the method |

## 2. Cold — the rules

**2.1 Qualify inside 2 seconds.** A cold parent must know within two seconds that this is for California parents of high schoolers and that it's about UC / college admissions. Either an eyebrow does it, or the first line of the headline does. If the eyebrow is dropped, the headline must name the topic (the 9/10 Guide v2 revision was entirely about this).

**2.2 Credibility on every unit.** The Design System credibility line — *UC Berkeley admissions reader · 10,000+ applications reviewed* — closes every cold asset. On photo treatments it may sit as a small badge above the headline instead. It is a quiet credential, never a giant stat block.

**2.3 Pick the ground by the ad's emotional job.**

| Emotional job | Ground (DS token) | Text | Typical hooks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authority / hard truth / quiet dread | **Deep** `--deep` (or True Black for maximum starkness) | White, emphasis in Yellow | "89% get rejected", "I never met them", "School says college" |
| Empathy / hope / reassurance | **Off White** `--off-white` | Deep / Black, emphasis Deep or `#C48B00` | "Don't change who they are", the calm version |
| Curiosity / urgency / punch | **Yellow** `--yellow` | Deep / Black only | Payoff cards, "find the kid" |

Never yellow type on a light ground (fails contrast) and never white type on yellow (DS legibility rules).

**2.4 Typography is the hero.** One headline, dominant, ~55% of the frame. One supporting device at most — a circled 4.0 under a dotted "4.2 average" line, a 2-vs-6-minute split, two pins on a California map, a closed folder. The device *proves* the headline; it never competes with it.

**2.5 Two treatments per concept.** Cold concepts are normally briefed as a pair:
- **Text-only** — typographic, no photography.
- **Photo** — Tony cut-out (DS rules: right of centre, standing on the frame floor, Deep-tinted drop shadow) + credibility badge; headline shifts left to clear him.

When a hook is a dramatic or mysterious scene ("60 seconds", "the check nobody tells you about"), the brief may say **no Tony** — the restraint is the point.

**2.6 Cold carousel architecture (4–5 cards).**

| Card | Role | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook — the only card that has to stop the scroll. Headline only + swipe cue | Dark |
| 2–3 | Agitate / reframe / the trap — "the viewer's own thoughts read back to them" | Light (content cards) |
| 4 | Turn + tease or the insider reveal — Tony appears here if he appears at all; teases are named, never explained | Light |
| 5 | Offer — free live training, CTA | Dark |

Dark bookends, light middle is the default rhythm. Some carousels deliberately run a **temperature arc** instead (warm photo → cold stark → warm resolution; or dense/tense card vs open/airy card). When a brief describes an arc, honour it: the contrast between cards *is* the message, so the cards must not look alike.

Every card works standalone. One idea per card. Progress dots top-right (active = Yellow), swipe cue bottom-right in muted grey.

**2.7 Cold — never.**
- Stock photos of stressed students, crying parents, campuses, laptops.
- Answering the "how" in the visual (no essays, applications or screens that show the method).
- Generic urgency imagery: alarm clocks, stopwatches, melting clocks, red sirens.
- The word "webinar" on the creative — it's a "free live training".
- Dates or times on the creative — ads are evergreen; the landing page handles logistics.

## 3. Warm — the rules

**3.1 Lead with the outcome.** The headline names results: schools, acceptances, five families. School names (Stanford, Berkeley, Cornell, USC, Carnegie Mellon) are proof and get weight — bold, or Deep/`#C48B00` emphasis on light grounds.

**3.2 Light, flat, editorial.** Off White ground, flat, no gradient, no texture. Mood: warm, aspirational, simple — "not an inspirational poster".

**3.3 One emotional anchor, or none.** Either a single flat illustration that carries the feeling (hands opening a yellow envelope, ACCEPTED stamp) or pure typography. Never photography of students; never decorative elements around the anchor.

**3.4 The italic-bold payoff line.** Warm creative uses a distinct italic Extra Bold line for the reversal or payoff ("They all got in.", "What does the reader need to see in 8 minutes?"). It must read visibly different from the list above it. Inter italic at 800 — no second family.

**3.5 Warm carousel architecture (4 cards).**

| Card | Role |
|---|---|
| 1 | Scroll-stopper — broken profiles → surprise reversal. The card that gets iterated |
| 2 | Mechanism — what the reader needs to see |
| 3 | Personal stakes — "does your kid follow the pattern or break it?" |
| 4 | CTA — Tony photo + compressed offer ("5 families. 1 pattern. 90 minutes.") + CTA |

Cards 1–3 text-only, centred. Tony only on card 4.

**3.6 Iterate, don't redesign.** A warm brief usually says "the visual is the winner". Treat the control file as a locked template: only the named copy zones change. Never re-break lines by shrinking type — add a line instead.

**3.7 Warm — never.**
- Fear or anxiety hooks paired with success visuals (tone clash — the briefs flag this repeatedly).
- Re-explaining who Tony is at length.
- Resolving the open loop ("what happened" stays in the training).

## 4. Translating brief language into Design System tokens

The 2026 briefs used several different hex values and fonts for the same roles. Map them as follows:

| Brief says | Use (DS) |
|---|---|
| Yellow / gold / amber — `#F5B800`, `#F5C200`, `#F5C518`, `#F5C842`, `#FABF32` | `--yellow` **#FABF32** |
| Navy / deep navy / near-black — `#1C2340`, `#1a1a2e`, `#0D1B2A`, `#0E0E0E`, `#1A1A1A`, `#1E1E00` | Dark ground → `--deep` **#1E1E00**. Use `--navy` #1B2340 only where the brief explicitly needs navy as a *distinct* field (e.g. a background-test round) |
| Black `#000000` (starkest editorial) | `--black` #000000 |
| Cream / warm off-white — `#F5F0E8`, `#F5F2ED`, `#FAF7F2`, `#F5F5F0`, light grey `#F8F8F8` | `--off-white` **#F8F8F8** |
| White | `--white` #FFFFFF |
| Muted grey sub-text on dark (`#9A9A9A`, `#CCCCCC`) | White at full strength for body on Deep (legibility floor); muted grey only for the swipe cue |
| Calibri, Montserrat, "bold sans" | **Inter** — 800 headlines, 400 body |
| ✅ / ➡️ emoji, Unicode ticks | Lucide `check` / `arrow-right` glyphs |
| Red X, red "NO" stamp, red bars, "red-tinted panic" | No red in the system. Use Navy for problem states, or invoke the DS "Apple rule" for a single realistic element **and flag it** in the delivery note |
| "Pill or rectangle" CTA, "full-width yellow rectangle" | Yellow pill, uppercase (DS) for new concepts. Iterations keep the control's shape. See open item 3 |

## 5. Iteration rounds (both temperatures)

Winning ads are re-tested in rounds. The 2026 Round 1 protocol:

**Round 1 — background only.** Produce versions on the brand grounds *other than* the control's. Copy frozen, font frozen, layout pixel-identical. Text and emphasis colours change only where the new ground requires it for legibility, and emphasis stays on the same words.

| Control ground | Produce |
|---|---|
| Deep | Off White · Yellow · Navy (if navy is an approved test field — open item 1) |
| Off White | Deep · Yellow · Navy |
| Yellow | Deep · Off White · Navy |

Ground swaps follow DS logo colourways (dual yellow/white on Deep/Navy, black on Yellow, full colour on Off White) and the legibility table in §2.3.

Also in iteration requests:
- Strip any date or time from older (e.g. 2025) creative before reuse.
- Resizes (e.g. to 1080×1080) re-flow inside the DS template; never stretch.

## 5B. Format specs (Meta)

| Placement | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Feed square (default) | 1080×1080 | Text and key visuals inside the centre 80% |
| Feed portrait | 1080×1350 | Preferred on mobile when specified |
| Landscape | 1200×628 | Only when specified |
| Stories / Reels | 1080×1920 or 1440×2560 | Keep the top 14% and bottom 20% clear; active area is the centre 66%. Only when requested |
| Carousel card | Same as feed | Up to 10 cards |

PNG for text-heavy ads; JPG acceptable when photo-dominant. Max 30MB. The `static-card` template is 1080×1080; other sizes re-flow its structure (briefed departure, noted on delivery). There is no 9:16 static template yet.

# PART 2B — Persona overlay

Source: *egelloC Persona Playbooks* and *egelloC Competitive Overview*. The playbooks were written for sales conversations, not ads. This part keeps what transfers to ad creative and leaves out pricing, tiers and close tactics, which never appear on top-of-funnel webinar creative.

**How the layers stack:** temperature (Part 2) decides the ad's job, then persona decides the vocabulary, the proof and the emotional register used to do that job. A cold ad for Persona 3 and a cold ad for Persona 1 have the same structure (qualify → credibility → open loop) but should not read alike.

## 6. The three personas, as they apply to ads

| | **P1 — First-gen immigrant family** | **P2 — 2nd-gen college-educated parent** | **P3 — Competitive California family** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | Parent 35–55, in the US 10–20 years, little or no US college experience. Student 9th–11th, often bilingual. SoCal (OC, LA, Inland Empire) or Bay Area suburbs | Parent 40–55, went to college 15–25 years ago (often UC/CSU). Student 10th–12th, solid but not exceptional GPA. Bay Area, SoCal suburbs, Sacramento | Parent 40–55, tech/finance/medicine/law. Student 9th–11th, 4.0+ weighted, targeting elite schools. Cupertino, Palo Alto, Fremont, Irvine, Arcadia, San Marino |
| Primary emotion | Fear / overwhelm — "I don't know what I don't know" | Uncertainty — "everything has changed since I applied" | Competitive pressure — "my kid looks good on paper, but so does everyone else" |
| C/A/C lead | All three equally; Clarity first | Clarity > Accountability > Connection | Connection (California expertise) > Accountability > Clarity |
| Register | Warm, patient, jargon-free | Professional, data-informed, respectful of what they know | Results-oriented, strategic, concise |
| Proof that lands | "Families just like yours", a sequenced path, a coach who knows your story | What's changed (then vs now), insider data, a systematic approach | California/zip-code specificity, insider edge, results data |
| Say | Step-by-step · you'll never feel lost · no question is too basic | Here's what's changed since you applied · UC Berkeley insider perspective · data-driven, systematic | Competitive edge · insider perspective · what UC readers look for from *your* zip code |
| Never say | Jargon (holistic admissions, demonstrated interest, yield rate) · assumed knowledge · prestige language ("Ivy League", "elite") · anything implying they should already know | Condescension · oversimplification · hard-sell urgency · emotion without evidence | "Accessible", "affordable" · overly warm/emotional language · comparisons to DIY or school counselors |

## 7. What each persona changes in the creative

These are applications of the playbooks to the rules in Part 2, not new rules. Where the brief says otherwise, the brief wins.

**P1 — First-gen immigrant family**
- Emotional job is usually **empathy / hope** → light ground (Off White). Dark "quiet dread" registers risk deepening the overwhelm this persona already feels.
- Favour sequence devices: steps, a roadmap, a timeline. Clarity is the product, so the visual should look like order arriving.
- Tony as **connection** (a guide who understands), not as a gatekeeper who rejected applications.
- Avoid walls of elite school names and logos; outcomes can be shown, but framed as "families like yours", not prestige.
- The overwhelm hook works here (Cold Script 1, "Lost in the System": chaos on one side, Tony and clarity on the other), but it must resolve in the same frame. Never leave this parent in the passive state.

**P2 — 2nd-gen college-educated parent**
- **Then-vs-now** is this persona's native device (the playbook's own example: UC acceptance rates around 40% when they applied vs single digits now). Data visuals, comparisons and the reader's process (8 minutes, 2 vs 6) all fit.
- Authority ground (Deep) is appropriate; the tone is "here's the update", not alarm.
- No countdowns, scarcity or urgency language. They are comparison shopping and resist pressure.
- Every emotional line needs a number or mechanism behind it.

**P3 — Competitive California family**
- Data and specificity: zip codes, named high schools (Monta Vista, Gunn, Lynbrook, University High, Troy), regional admit rates, "2 out of 30", the 860,000 sea of sameness. The 2026 "Beyond the GPA" briefs are the reference set for this persona.
- Authority ground (Deep or True Black), editorial, restrained. Minimal emotional imagery; no acceptance-moment sentimentality.
- School names and elite targets are fine and expected.
- Never position against DIY or school counselors; the frame is "everyone at your school is equally qualified".

## 8. Persona × temperature

| | Cold | Warm |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Qualify gently, no jargon. Overwhelm → clear path. Light ground, sequence device, Tony as guide | "Families like yours got in." Outcomes without prestige framing; the coach relationship visible |
| **P2** | "Here's what's changed." Then-vs-now data on an authority ground | Before/after case studies with the mechanism named (the Pattern format fits well) |
| **P3** | Zip-code and sameness data; insider reveal; editorial dark | Results and school names; "what made 2 out of 30 stand out" |
| **All personas** | Write to the most specific persona the campaign can support. If a brief must target all three, default to P2 register: data-informed, no jargon, no prestige language, no urgency | The 5-families case studies brief is the 2026 all-persona reference |

**Coverage gap worth noting:** in the 2026 webinar briefs, P3 has its own campaign ("Beyond the GPA") and the cold admissions briefs mostly read as P2/P3, but no webinar brief was written specifically for P1, the persona the company mission names first.

## 9. Proof claims — use the qualified wording

Results figures carry qualifiers in the source documents and must keep them on creative.

| Figure | Source wording | Seen on 2026 creative |
|---|---|---|
| 98.4% | Long-term students (4+ years) admitted to one of their **top-10 choices** | "98.4% of our students get into a **top-10 school**" (Case Studies, CF briefs) · "98.4% top-10 admission rate" |
| 93.1% | Long-term students admitted to one of their **top-5 choices** | — |

"A top-10 school" (a ranking) and "one of their top-10 choices" (their own list) are different claims. Creative should say "one of their top-10 choices" and should not drop "long-term" where space allows. Flag any brief that shortens these. (Cold Script 3, "98% of our students got into one of their top 10 colleges", is closer but still omits the long-term qualifier.)

# PART 3 — Writing a brief Claude Design can execute

## 10. Every brief starts with this header

```
CAMPAIGN:            [webinar name — e.g. "Beyond the GPA" free live training]
AUDIENCE TEMPERATURE: COLD | WARM        ← required, one word, no other use of "warm"
PERSONA:             P1 first-gen immigrant | P2 2nd-gen college-educated | P3 competitive CA | ALL
AUDIENCE:            [who, grade, state, region]
AWARENESS STAGE:     [unaware | problem-aware | solution-aware | product-aware]
BRIEF TYPE:          NEW CONCEPT | COPY ITERATION | BACKGROUND ROUND | RESIZE
CONTROL FILE:        [link — required for any iteration or round]
DESTINATION:         [registration page URL]
FORMAT / SIZE:       [1080×1080 default · 1080×1350 · 1440×2560 9:16 with safe zones]
UNITS:               [list: S1 static (text-only + photo), C1 carousel 5 cards, …]
SOURCE SCRIPT:       [script ID, if from the video-ad batch]
ASSUMPTIONS:         [anything inferred — mark "Confirm"]
```

Followed by a short **Brand reference** section: Design System link, logo colourways, credibility line, Tony assets available.

**Inferred temperature.** The brief writer may assume COLD for an egelloC free live training when the temperature wasn't given, marked "COLD (Confirm)". Claude Design builds a brief marked that way as cold and repeats the flag in the delivery note. A direct ad request with no brief and no temperature still gets asked in the intake form.

## 11. Then one block per unit

```
UNIT [ID] — [STATIC | CAROUSEL] — "[working name]"

STRATEGY
  Angle:              [myth-bust / stat shock / insider mystery / recognition / outcome …]
  Emotional job:      [authority-hard-truth | empathy-hope | curiosity-urgency]   (cold)
                      [outcome-hope | surprise-reversal]                         (warm)
  Trigger:            [fear of loss | curiosity | hope | trust | urgency | surprise | relief | guilt (light)]
  Awareness:          [unaware | problem-aware | solution-aware | product-aware | most aware]
  Job of this ad:     [one sentence — e.g. stop scroll + open loop on "how it's built"]

COPY — set verbatim
  Eyebrow:            [text | none]
  Headline:           [text, with [brackets] around the emphasis words]
  Sub / body:         [text]
  Payoff line:        [text | none]          (warm: italic bold)
  Credibility:        [default line | badge | none — cold must not be none]
  CTA:                [text]

VISUAL
  Ground (role):      [dark | light | brand]   — not a hex
  Treatment:          [text-only | Tony photo | both | illustration | mockup]
  Tony usage:         [none | photo treatment | card N only]
  Supporting device:  [one item, or none]
  Layout:             [zones, proportions, alignment — e.g. headline ~55%]
  Typography:         [size dominance, emphasis words, body relative to headline]
  Brand:              [logo colourway, credibility line/badge, CTA]
  Mood:               [2–3 precise adjectives + what to avoid]
  Hierarchy:          1. … 2. … 3. … 4. CTA

SKETCH
  [ASCII layout — required for every unit and every carousel card; ground role noted above it]

DO NOT
  - [the specific mistakes this concept invites]
```

For a **carousel**, replace COPY with a card table: `Card | Role | Copy | Ground | Swipe/CTA`, and note any temperature arc in one line.

For a **copy iteration**, list only the zones that change per version and say "everything else matches the control".

## 12. Conventions that save a revision round

- **Emphasis in [brackets].** Only bracketed words get the accent treatment. One to three words per headline.
- **Name the ground by role, not hex.** "Dark ground" → Claude Design picks Deep. Hexes in briefs have drifted across six yellows and six darks in 2026.
- **Say "free live training", never "webinar"**, in on-creative copy.
- **No dates or times** on creative.
- **One idea per unit.** If a static needs two paragraphs, it's a carousel.
- **Cold: put the audience and the topic in the first line or the eyebrow.** Cold: always state the credibility treatment. Warm: name the control file.
- **Write the "Do not" list.** It is the most useful part of the 2026 briefs — it's where the specific failure modes of each concept get blocked.
- **Name one persona.** Copy register, proof and "never say" lists differ sharply between P1 and P3 (Part 2B). "All personas" defaults to P2 register.
- **Proof claims keep their qualifiers** — "one of their top-10 choices", long-term students (§9).
- **Mark Tony usage explicitly** (none / photo treatment / card N only). Unstated, it defaults to the rules in Part 2.

## 13. Pre-send checklist

- [ ] Audience temperature stated as COLD or WARM
- [ ] Persona stated; copy checked against that persona's "never say" list
- [ ] Results figures use the qualified wording (§9)
- [ ] Brief type stated; control file linked for iterations
- [ ] Every unit has copy, emphasis marked, ground role, treatment, hierarchy, Do-not list
- [ ] Cold: audience + topic visible in 2 seconds; credibility on every unit
- [ ] Warm: outcome-first headline; no fear hook paired with a success visual
- [ ] No "webinar", no dates/times, no emoji, no stock student photos
- [ ] Sizes listed; 9:16 requests mention safe zones

## 14. Open items — defaults until decided

These are not blockers. Where a brief specifies one of them, follow the brief (§0, "Guardrails, not a box"). Where it's silent, use the current DS default.

1. **Background-round colour fields.** Rounds specify Navy `#1a1a2e`, warm off-white `#FAF7F2`, yellow `#F5C200`, near-black. The DS palette is Deep `#1E1E00`, Off White `#F8F8F8`, Yellow `#FABF32`, Navy `#1B2340`. This file maps them to DS tokens. Confirm, or update the DS if these fields are intentional.
2. **Credibility line on warm creative.** The DS requires it on every ad-facing asset; the warm winners omit it. Current default: follow the control, flag it.
3. **CTA shape.** The DS specifies pills; the warm winners use a full-width yellow rectangle. Current default: pill for new concepts, keep the control's shape for iterations.
4. **Headline casing.** Several cold briefs set headlines in all caps; the DS says sentence case on statics. Current default: DS sentence case, flag it.
5. **9:16 statics.** The Summer Planning brief asks for 1440×2560 with safe zones; the DS has no 9:16 static template yet.
6. **Majors Webinar and Winner Ads Fast Iterations** — confirm they ran to cold audiences.
7. **Results claims.** Several 2026 briefs state "98.4% get into a top-10 school"; the source figure is long-term students reaching one of their top-10 *choices*. Confirm the approved on-creative wording.
8. **Brand voice vs P3.** The Design System voice is "warm and direct"; the P3 playbook says avoid overly warm language. Suggested reading: warm means human, not sentimental. Confirm.
9. **No P1 webinar campaign yet** — intentional, or a gap to brief?
